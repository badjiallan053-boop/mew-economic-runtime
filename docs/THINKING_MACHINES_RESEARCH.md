# Thinking Machines research: practical adaptation for MEW

Reviewed 7 October 2026. The [machine-readable catalog](../research/learning/thinking-machines-catalog.json) records twelve primary sources and individual access/reuse boundaries. Official blog/documentation text and repository README/license text were accessible. Paper abstracts and targeted LoRA/DPO HTML method sections were read; full PDFs and entire papers were not reviewed. The official YouTube channel link failed to load, so no video claims or transcripts inform this report. No cloud training, model upload or external-text training export occurred.

## Hosted infrastructure and reusable methods are different

[Tinker](https://thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/) is a managed training API, not an installed local GPU engine. The [announcement](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/announcing-tinker/) explicitly describes provider-operated clusters and an open-source cookbook built on that service. Its historical free-start language is not a current pricing commitment. Current model availability differs from launch examples; recheck support before any proposed run.

The [Tinker Cookbook](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook) exposes useful experiment abstractions and examples, but executing its training clients calls the hosted service. Its [Apache-2.0 license](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/blob/main/LICENSE) governs repository code. It does not approve every example dataset, hosted-service use, base-model weight or copied media text. MEW retains its isolated local Qwen/MLX path; remote Tinker credentials, an explicit training budget and data-transfer decisions remain pending. The previously approved Railway budget does not authorize GPU training expenses.

## What transfers to our failures

| --- | --- | --- |
| [LoRA Without Regret](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/) | Experiments sweep learning rates, ranks, module placement and batch sizes; adapter capacity and optimization choices affect comparisons with full fine-tuning. | Record actual MLX target modules, rank/scaling, batch size and learning rate. Use a small validation sweep rather than copying a purported universal optimum. Publisher results on other model sizes/data do not prove quantized Qwen3-0.6B will improve. |
| [On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/) | Student trajectories receive teacher token-probability feedback, addressing states reached by the student; imitation can copy confidence without factual accuracy. | Preserve MEW's real failed traces for error discovery. Corrected demonstrations or teacher-guided training require independently checked grounding and affirmative output reuse. Token-level teacher probabilities and a compatible training implementation are prerequisites; no teacher was invoked here. |
| [Defeating Nondeterminism](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/) | Batch-size-dependent numerical reductions can change outputs, even beyond sampling variation. | Temperature zero and a seed are reproducibility controls, not a proof of deterministic inference. Record device, runtime, batching, model revision and raw outputs; repeat selected cases to measure instability. CUDA kernel work is not a verified MLX patch. |
| [Expert judgment case study](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/learning-to-replicate-expert-judgment-in-financial-tasks/) | A collaborator reports correcting contested training labels with experts and evaluating on a distinct holdout; training recipe ablations are reported. | Build a genuine domain-review queue, preserve contested examples and reviewer provenance, and keep holdout families disjoint. The case study's proprietary dataset, larger model and reported performance do not establish MEW performance or investment ability. |
| [Tinker preference guide](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/cookbook/preferences/) | Distinguishes direct chosen/rejected preference optimization from reward-model-plus-RL training. | Preference records must bind the same question/context and state why one response is better. A model preference is not authenticated human approval. Verify a local implementation before proposing DPO; the hosted example is not an MLX drop-in. |
| [Original LoRA paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685) | Abstract describes learning low-rank updates while freezing pretrained weights. | LoRA is a parameterization, not a substitute for a good dataset or a guarantee of better answers. Continue versioned base-versus-adapter comparisons. |
| [Original DPO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290) | Abstract presents preference learning without fitting a separate reward model. | Consider only after accurate demonstrations, meaningful pairwise feedback and disjoint evaluation exist. JSON-valid but wrong answers must remain rejected regardless of style preference. |
| [Original RAG paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401) | Abstract combines retrieval memory with generation for knowledge tasks. | Missing French evidence is a retrieval problem first. Evaluate multilingual passage retrieval and final context coverage before tuning answer weights. |
| [Who Validates the Validators?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272) | Abstract studies alignment of LLM-assisted evaluation with human preferences. | Calibrate subjective judges against real expert labels; keep syntax/citation/authority checks separate from subjective usefulness. ## Method choice: staged rather than interchangeable

1. **RAG and multilingual retrieval:** use when evidence is absent, stale or inaccessible in the model context. Save source/chunk hashes and measure exact-span coverage; preserve qualifiers such as unknown, verified and preprod. More weight training cannot recover an omitted passage reliably.
2. **SFT with a local LoRA adapter:** consider when evidence is present but the model repeatedly violates a stable response convention or documented advisory behavior. Only train from reuse-approved, accurately labeled, disjoint examples. Token masking, truncation and chat rendering must match the real inference path. The existing [MLX LoRA guide](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/LORA.md) is the local compatibility reference; cookbook renderers and checkpoint formats need explicit translation, not assumption.
3. **DPO:** consider later for judged alternatives under identical context, such as useful versus evasive grounded explanations. Do not optimize preferences that reward convincing fabricated citations. Treat any unverified local DPO implementation as pending.
4. **RL or on-policy distillation:** reserve for environments with validated rewards/teachers and a demonstrated failure that simpler corrections do not solve. Schema compliance alone is an easily incomplete reward. Any experimental economic environment must be a simulation; trained policies never replace deterministic monetary authorization.

Personalization should mean an explicitly approved organization's vocabulary, response conventions and relevant evidence, scoped by tenant and retention rules. It must not silently ingest private customer traces, user secrets or arbitrary web text. Learning organizational judgment needs representative expert feedback; paper text and blog summaries are research inputs, not training labels.

## Required evaluation receipt

Each candidate should bind a base-model revision and license record, adapter/configuration hash, retrieval snapshot, renderer/tokenizer revision, runtime/device, data lineage, reviewer identity/type and measured language slices. Report source retrieval, span presence, parse/schema validity, citation entailment, answer correctness, abstention and authority violations independently. Compare the same cases with the same retrieval context when measuring an adapter; evaluate retrieval changes in their own comparison.

No similarity score or model confidence establishes authenticated evidence. Calibrate abstention on reviewed answerable and unanswerable cases; report false acceptance as well as excessive refusal. Twelve repeatedly used development cases cannot support customer generalization or robust calibration. Fresh pilot questions and real human review remain necessary.

The immediate useful transfer from Thinking Machines is careful experiment design, improved label review and an explicit separation of data, optimization and evaluation. It is not evidence that its hosted stack, larger models or reported results were integrated into MEW.

## Targeted paper extraction

Original method notes are in [posttraining-method-notes.json](../research/learning/posttraining-method-notes.json). LoRA section 4 describes frozen base weights plus a trainable low-rank update, with zero initial update and explicit scaling. MEW must check MLX parameter conventions rather than equating implementations by name. DPO sections 3–4 use same-prompt preference pairs and a reference policy in a relative log-probability loss. The preference assumptions and reference distribution matter; stylistic preference cannot certify factual correctness. These are original summaries for experiment planning, not paper text exported for training.
